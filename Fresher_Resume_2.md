## ACADEMIC PROJECTS

### **PROJECT 1: Electricity Generation Using Waste Material**
**Duration:** February 2025 - May 2025 | **Team Size:** 4

**Objective:**  
To design and develop a waste-to-energy system that generates electricity through thermal energy conversion while reducing environmental pollution using pollution control filters.

**Description:**
- Designed combustion chamber for controlled burning of waste materials
- Implemented heating process to convert waste into thermal energy (heat)
- Integrated pollution control filter (activated carbon) to reduce smoke and harmful emissions
- Installed heating sensors (K-type thermocouples) to monitor temperature levels
- Used thermoelectric heating panels (Peltier modules - TEC1-12706) to convert heat into electricity
- Implemented DC-DC boost converter for voltage regulation
- Connected battery storage system for continuous power supply

**Results:**
- Generated 10-15 watts of electrical power from waste heat
- Achieved operating temperature range of 150-250°C
- Pollution filter reduced smoke emissions by approximately 60%
- Successfully powered LED lights and charged small DC devices
- Demonstrated dual benefit: waste management + renewable energy
- Presented at college technical exhibition

**Components Used:**  
Combustion Chamber, Pollution Filter, Thermocouples, Thermoelectric Panels, Boost Converter, Battery (12V, 7Ah)

### **PROJECT 2: Electricity Generation Using Speed Breaker**
**Duration:** November 2024 - January 2025 | **Team Size:** 4

**Objective:**  
To harness kinetic energy from vehicles passing over speed breakers and convert it into electrical energy for practical applications.

**Description:**
- Designed and installed rack and pinion gear mechanism beneath speed breaker
- Vertical pressure from vehicles converted to rotary motion through gears
- Connected DC generator (12V, 5W) to rotating shaft for electricity generation
- Integrated spring return system for mechanism reset
- Connected battery storage (12V, 7Ah) for energy accumulation
- Added voltage regulator for stable output

**Results:**
- Generated 8-10 watts per vehicle crossing
- Successfully stored energy in rechargeable battery
- Powered LED street lights for demonstration
- Estimated 100 vehicles/hour can generate 50-60 Wh
- Demonstrated at college project exhibition

**Components Used:**  
Rack & Pinion Mechanism, DC Generator, Springs, Battery, Voltage Regulator, Metal Frame
